Electrolysis in Valencia

How electrolysis works

A very fine needle is inserted into each follicle and delivers a discharge that destroys the hair's growth zone. The method treats every hair individually — so colour is irrelevant: blond, red, grey and fine hair comes out just as dark hair does.

With correctly chosen parameters and clean technique, a treated follicle no longer produces hair. It is the only method with that outcome, but it takes time: only a limited number of hairs can be treated per minute, which is why the work is charged by the minute.

Parameters chosen for you

The strength of the discharge, the contact time and the needle type are set for you: for the thickness of the hair, the depth of the follicle, your skin type and sensitivity. The same settings do not suit a chin and a shin, so parameters change as the work goes on — the specialist follows your skin's reaction rather than one fixed mode.

Four to ten hands at once

Several specialists can work on one area at the same time. What normally stretches over hours and several visits is finished several times faster. The price list has a separate four-hands option of 120 minutes.

Who it suits

  • blond, red, grey and fine hair that the laser cannot see
  • the face: chin, cheeks, upper lip, neck
  • single hairs left after a course of laser
  • the body: any area, including bikini and underarms
  • cases where the hair needs to be gone for good, not just thinned

How a session goes

  1. checking the area and contraindications, setting the parameters
  2. numbing: a topical cream, other options if needed
  3. treating hairs one by one: short pinpoint stings with warmth
  4. antiseptic and a soothing product
  5. charged by the device timer, for the time actually worked

How the course is built

A hair can only be treated when it is visible and in its active phase. Sessions therefore run 1–4 weeks apart: more often at first, then less as the amount of hair drops.

The number of sessions is always individual and depends on the area, hair density and type, hormones, and what has been done to the area before — years of plucking and waxing distort the follicle and make the work harder. As a guide: the face takes 10–20 sessions, the body 15–30 or more. That is a guide, not a promise.

Between sessions, no plucking and no waxing: the hair has to grow to a visible length, otherwise there is nothing to treat. Shaving is fine.

How to prepare

  • the hair must be visible: 1–3 mm, which is 3–5 days after shaving
  • no plucking, waxing or sugaring for 2–4 weeks
  • no tanning in the days before the session
  • no creams or deodorant on the area on the day
  • tell us about medication and any skin conditions

Aftercare

Honestly about how skin reacts: after electrolysis there is almost always redness and pinpoint swelling around the treated follicles. Some clients develop small crusts — a normal part of healing, and we cannot promise they will not appear: the reaction depends on your skin, the area and how many hairs were treated.

  • redness lasts from a few hours up to a day or two
  • any crusts fall away on their own within 5–10 days — do not pick them or marks can remain
  • 2–3 days without sauna, pool or intense training
  • do not touch the area on the first day and keep make-up off a treated face
  • antiseptic as advised, then a healing product
  • SPF on exposed areas while the skin recovers

Contraindications

  • pregnancy and breastfeeding
  • a pacemaker or other electronic implants
  • epilepsy
  • cancer
  • severe diabetes, blood clotting disorders
  • inflammation, wounds or breakouts in the treated area
  • a tendency to keloid scarring

Parameters and contraindications are checked before the work starts. The price list includes a 30-minute trial on the body — it shows how your own skin responds.

Laser and electrolysis are not rivals

They are two different tools. Laser quickly clears the bulk of the hair; electrolysis finishes what laser cannot reach. The right answer is almost never “either, or” — it is “first one, then the other”.

Laser sees pigment

The beam is absorbed by the melanin inside the hair and heats the follicle. That is why laser handles dark, dense hair so well and covers large areas in one session: full legs take about an hour. Grey, red and fine light hair carries almost no melanin, so the laser simply does not see it, however many sessions you book.

Electrolysis treats every hair separately

A very fine probe is placed into the follicle and a current destroys the growth zone. Hair colour is irrelevant — the method works the same on grey, blonde and vellus hair. You pay for that in time: the specialist works hair by hair, so electrolysis is impractical for large areas, while for the face, areolas and single stubborn hairs it is the only way to remove them permanently.

The usual route is laser first, electrolysis after

Start with a laser course: 6–8 sessions, 4–6 weeks apart on the body and 3–4 weeks on the face. It removes 80–90 % of the hair. What remains is what the laser does not take: individual light hairs and hairs that lightened during the course. Electrolysis picks those up — short sessions every few weeks, not another long course.

What we will tell you at the consultation

We look at hair colour and density, skin type and hormonal background, and tell you honestly what laser will do and what will have to be finished with a probe. If your hair is light to begin with, we will not sell you a laser course — that is money thrown away. You learn the number of sessions and the intervals before you pay for the first one.

Common questions

Does it hurt?
It feels like a short pinpoint sting with warmth. Tolerance varies: the body is easier, the face and bikini more sensitive. We use topical numbing and set the parameters to your threshold. It is not a painless method, and we say so upfront.
How many sessions will I need?
The face usually takes 10–20 sessions and the body 15–30 or more, spaced 1–4 weeks apart. The exact number depends on the area, hair density, hormones and whether the area was plucked in the past. It is a guide, not a fixed promise.
Can blond and grey hair be removed?
Yes. Electrolysis does not depend on pigment: the needle works on the follicle itself, so hair colour is irrelevant. That is the key difference from laser.
Will there be crusts?
There can be — a normal part of healing, particularly on the face and with coarse hair. They clear in 5–10 days and must not be picked. Promising no crusts at all would not be honest: skin reacts differently in everyone.
Will it leave marks or scars?
Not with correct parameters and aftercare. The risk rises if crusts are picked, if fresh skin is tanned, or if the work is done on an inflamed area — which is why we do not treat an irritated area.
Do I pay per area or per time?
Per time — by the minute, on the device timer, for the time actually worked. It is fairer: the same area holds a different amount of hair on different people.
Can it be combined with laser?
Yes, and it often is: the laser clears the bulk of the dark hair, electrolysis handles the blond, grey and remaining single hairs. We set the order at the consultation.

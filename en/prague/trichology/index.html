Trichology in Prague

What people come with

Most often with shedding that has lasted more than a month. But scalp problems go wider: itching, flaking, dandruff, greasy roots, a tight feeling, thinning hair and lost volume. These are often connected, and it makes sense to start with the scalp rather than with another new shampoo.

  • hair shedding more than usual for over a month
  • volume has dropped, the hair has become finer
  • itching, flaking, dandruff, crusts
  • greasy roots with dry lengths
  • shedding after childbirth, illness, a strict diet or stress

What happens at the appointment

  1. a conversation: when it started, what changed in your health, what you take
  2. trichoscopy: the scalp and hair examined under magnification, area by area
  3. assessment: hair density, follicle condition, the ratio of fine to thick hairs, inflammation
  4. going through home care: shampoo, washing frequency, styling, colouring
  5. a first treatment and a plan: care, intervals and, if needed, medical tests

Where care ends and medicine begins

We work with the scalp: cleansing, settling inflammation, care, treatments that feed the follicles. That is the cosmetic side, and it genuinely helps — but only when the cause of the shedding lies in the scalp or in your routine.

If the shedding is driven by iron deficiency, the thyroid, hormonal change, medication or a skin condition, no treatment will remove the cause. In that case we say plainly that tests and a doctor are needed, and only then choose the care — so that you do not pay for a course that cannot work.

The care plan

After the assessment we set out a plan: which treatments and how far apart, what to change at home, when to come back for a review. Results are judged on trichoscopy after 2–3 months — earlier is not meaningful: hair grows slowly, and change only becomes visible over a full cycle.

Trichoscopy: the scalp under magnification

We look at the scalp and hair area by area and assess density and the state of the follicles. In 2–3 months we compare the images — that shows whether the plan is working.

The first appointment: we look at the scalp under magnification, work through the causes and carry out the first treatment there and then.

A trichoscope shows what the eye cannot: the state of the follicular openings, hair density in a given area, the ratio of fine to thick hairs, inflammation, flaking and the condition of the scalp. That picture tells us what we are dealing with: temporary shedding after stress or illness, hair thinning, seborrhoeic changes, or something that belongs with a doctor.

The line matters: scalp care is our area, while diagnosing and treating disease is the doctor’s. If we see signs that call for tests or prescriptions, we say so plainly and recommend a work-up. Running treatments blind, when the cause may sit in hormones, iron or the thyroid, is pointless: the money goes and the shedding continues.

How it goes

  1. a conversation: when it started, what changed in your life and health, what medication you take
  2. examining the scalp and hair with a trichoscope, area by area
  3. going through home care: shampoo, washing frequency, styling, colouring
  4. a first scalp treatment based on what we find
  5. a plan: care, intervals and, if needed, a medical work-up

Who it suits

After the treatment

How often. The first appointment once, then a review after 2–3 months. Scalp treatments usually run as a course, with the interval set by the condition.

A review: we compare the picture with the first visit and adjust the plan. Without a second look there is no way to tell whether what we are doing works.

The trichologist examines the same areas with the trichoscope again and compares: has density changed, are there fewer fine hairs, has the inflammation and flaking settled. If you have test results, we go through those too.

Then we either continue the course or change the approach: sometimes adjusting home care is enough, sometimes it is a matter of waiting, and sometimes it means going back to the doctor. If nothing has changed at all, we will say so plainly rather than extend a course for its own sake.

Mesotherapy

Dermapen scalp mesotherapy45 minutes4500 Kč

The scalp cocktail goes in through micro-channels made by a Dermapen, with no syringe injections. We only do it after a trichoscopy.

A device with a sterile single-use tip passes along the partings in the area of thinning or shedding, creating micro-channels at a set depth. The cocktail enters the scalp through them. The trichologist sets the depth by area and by the state of the skin.

Two mechanisms work together: the formula itself and the skin’s response to controlled micro-injury, which increases blood flow around the follicles. The cocktail is chosen from the trichoscopy findings: some formulas address follicle nutrition, others oiliness and inflammation of the scalp.

What matters: the treatment does not remove the cause of the shedding. If it comes from iron deficiency, hormones or disease, the effect is temporary and the shedding returns. That is why we examine with a trichoscope first and refer for tests when needed. Results are judged no earlier than three months in — that is the hair cycle.

How it goes

  1. trichoscopy and choosing the cocktail
  2. antiseptic preparation of the scalp
  3. passing the Dermapen along the partings in the area
  4. applying the formula
  5. care guidance for the next day or two

Who it suits

  • thinning hair and lower density
  • shedding confirmed by examination
  • an oily or inflamed scalp
  • maintenance after a period of shedding
  • you would rather avoid syringe injections

After the treatment

  • you feel tingling and sometimes stinging during the session — bearable, but not painless
  • redness and a tender scalp until the evening, sometimes into the next day
  • do not wash your hair for 24 hours
  • two days without sauna, pool or hard training
  • not done with inflammation or wounds on the scalp, in pregnancy, with clotting disorders or a tendency to keloid scarring

How often. Usually a course of 6–10 sessions 1–2 weeks apart, then maintenance. The trichologist sets the exact course after the examination.

Common questions

Why does hair fall out?
There are many causes and usually several at once: iron and vitamin D deficiency, thyroid problems, hormonal change including after childbirth, stress and poor sleep, strict diets, recent illness, certain medications, and the condition of the scalp itself. That is why we start with an examination rather than by booking treatments.
When should I see a trichologist?
If heavier shedding has lasted more than a month, if volume has visibly dropped, if there is itching, flaking or crusting, or if shedding started after childbirth, illness or rapid weight loss. The sooner the better: holding on to density is easier than bringing back follicles that are already gone.
Do I need blood tests?
Not always, but often. If trichoscopy shows a picture typical of deficiencies or hormonal causes, going further without tests is pointless. We will say which ones at the appointment; usually ferritin, vitamin D, thyroid markers and a full blood count.
When can I expect results?
The first changes show after 2–3 months, a clear result after 4–6. That is not the clinic being slow — it is the hair cycle: new hair grows about a centimetre a month. Anyone promising results in two weeks is talking about how the lengths look, not about the roots.
Will scalp mesotherapy help?
It works well as part of a plan once the cause is clear. As the only measure against iron deficiency or a thyroid problem it gives a temporary effect and the shedding returns. That is why we prescribe it after an examination rather than on request.
Should I wash my hair before the appointment?
Come with your hair in its usual state — better not to wash it on the day. That way the real condition of the scalp, the oiliness at the roots and any flaking are visible.

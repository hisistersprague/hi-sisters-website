Laser and electrolysis are not rivals

They are two different tools. Laser quickly clears the bulk of the hair; electrolysis finishes what laser cannot reach. The right answer is almost never “either, or” — it is “first one, then the other”.

Laser sees pigment

The beam is absorbed by the melanin inside the hair and heats the follicle. That is why laser handles dark, dense hair so well and covers large areas in one session: full legs take about an hour. Grey, red and fine light hair carries almost no melanin, so the laser simply does not see it, however many sessions you book.

Electrolysis treats every hair separately

A very fine probe is placed into the follicle and a current destroys the growth zone. Hair colour is irrelevant — the method works the same on grey, blonde and vellus hair. You pay for that in time: the specialist works hair by hair, so electrolysis is impractical for large areas, while for the face, areolas and single stubborn hairs it is the only way to remove them permanently.

The usual route is laser first, electrolysis after

Start with a laser course: 6–8 sessions, 4–6 weeks apart on the body and 3–4 weeks on the face. It removes 80–90 % of the hair. What remains is what the laser does not take: individual light hairs and hairs that lightened during the course. Electrolysis picks those up — short sessions every few weeks, not another long course.

What we will tell you at the consultation

We look at hair colour and density, skin type and hormonal background, and tell you honestly what laser will do and what will have to be finished with a probe. If your hair is light to begin with, we will not sell you a laser course — that is money thrown away. You learn the number of sessions and the intervals before you pay for the first one.

Laser epilation in Prague

Laser epilation is the most effective method for permanent removal of dark hair. The diode laser targets melanin, destroys the follicle with thermal energy without damaging surrounding skin.

How the laser works

The laser beam is absorbed by melanin, the pigment inside the hair. That energy turns into heat and damages the follicle. The darker and thicker the hair, the more melanin it holds and the better the method works.

One detail shapes the whole course: the laser only acts on hairs in the active growth phase. At any given moment only part of the hair in an area is in that phase — the rest is dormant and will not respond at that session. So a single treatment cannot solve the task in principle, and that has nothing to do with the device.

Who it suits

The method works best on dark hair. Blond, red and grey hair does not hold enough melanin — electrolysis is more effective there, and we will say so plainly rather than sell a course that will not work.

  • dark body hair: underarms, bikini, legs, arms, back, chest
  • ingrown hairs and irritation from shaving
  • men's areas: back, chest, shoulders, neck, abdomen
  • we do not laser a woman's face — facial hair in women is more often hormone-driven and the laser can stimulate growth there; for the face we offer electrolysis

How a session goes

  1. checking the area and contraindications, setting parameters for your skin and hair
  2. protective goggles, applying gel
  3. passing the handpiece over the area: it feels like a short hot flick, the cooled tip softens it
  4. a soothing product afterwards
  5. 15–60 minutes depending on the area

How the course is built

We work towards a result, not towards endless maintenance sessions. The course is spaced to catch different groups of hair in their growth phase: the first sessions are about 4–6 weeks apart, then the interval grows — 8, 10, 12 weeks and more.

A longer interval is not us stretching the course; it is the logic of it. The fewer hairs remain, the longer it takes for the rest to enter the active phase. Coming earlier is pointless: the session would largely be wasted.

Most people see clear thinning after 2–3 sessions, and a full course usually runs 6–10 sessions or more, depending on the area, hormones, hair density and colour. That is why we do not quote a fixed number of sessions in advance. But if laser hair removal is right for you, we guarantee the result and see the course through: at the consultation the specialist will tell you plainly whether the method suits you, and if it does not, she will offer electrolysis rather than sell a course on spec.

After a course some clients see hair partly return, usually alongside hormonal change: pregnancy, starting or stopping medication, thyroid conditions. That is not a failure of the course but how the body works; in those cases single maintenance sessions are enough.

How to prepare

  • shave the area 12–24 hours before: the hair should be in the follicle, not above the skin
  • no sun, sunbeds or fake tan for two weeks beforehand
  • no plucking, waxing or sugaring for 2–4 weeks: the follicle has to be there
  • no creams, deodorant or oils on the area on the day
  • tell us about your medication: some drugs increase light sensitivity

Aftercare

  • redness and slight swelling around the follicles is a normal reaction and settles within hours
  • no sauna, pool or hot bath for 1–2 days
  • no sun or sunbeds for two weeks; SPF on exposed areas is a must
  • do not scrub the area with a washcloth or exfoliant for 3–4 days
  • after 1–2 weeks the hairs start to shed: that is expected, a gentle scrub helps
  • deodorant after underarms — from the next day

Contraindications

  • pregnancy and breastfeeding
  • a fresh tan, including fake tan
  • cancer, epilepsy
  • wounds, inflammation or breakouts in the treated area
  • medication that increases light sensitivity, including isotretinoin
  • moles and tattoos in the area: we work around them
  • active herpes when treating the face or bikini

Laser and electrolysis are not rivals

They are two different tools. Laser quickly clears the bulk of the hair; electrolysis finishes what laser cannot reach. The right answer is almost never “either, or” — it is “first one, then the other”.

Laser sees pigment

The beam is absorbed by the melanin inside the hair and heats the follicle. That is why laser handles dark, dense hair so well and covers large areas in one session: full legs take about an hour. Grey, red and fine light hair carries almost no melanin, so the laser simply does not see it, however many sessions you book.

Electrolysis treats every hair separately

A very fine probe is placed into the follicle and a current destroys the growth zone. Hair colour is irrelevant — the method works the same on grey, blonde and vellus hair. You pay for that in time: the specialist works hair by hair, so electrolysis is impractical for large areas, while for the face, areolas and single stubborn hairs it is the only way to remove them permanently.

The usual route is laser first, electrolysis after

Start with a laser course: 6–8 sessions, 4–6 weeks apart on the body and 3–4 weeks on the face. It removes 80–90 % of the hair. What remains is what the laser does not take: individual light hairs and hairs that lightened during the course. Electrolysis picks those up — short sessions every few weeks, not another long course.

What we will tell you at the consultation

We look at hair colour and density, skin type and hormonal background, and tell you honestly what laser will do and what will have to be finished with a probe. If your hair is light to begin with, we will not sell you a laser course — that is money thrown away. You learn the number of sessions and the intervals before you pay for the first one.

FAQ

Do you guarantee the result?
Yes — if laser hair removal is right for you. At the consultation we look at the type and colour of your hair, the condition of your skin, and whether hormones are driving the growth. If the method suits you, we see the course through to the result. If it does not, we say so immediately and offer electrolysis, which works on hair of any colour.
Does laser hair removal hurt?
It feels like a short hot flick. The cooled tip softens it noticeably. The most sensitive areas are those with thin skin — bikini and underarms; the first sessions are sharper, then it gets easier as there is less hair.
How many sessions will I need?
Usually 6–10, sometimes more: the laser only works on hair in the active growth phase and that share differs from person to person, so we do not quote a fixed number in advance. Thinning is usually visible after 2–3 sessions, and we guarantee the result if the method suits you.
Why do the intervals between sessions get longer?
Because each time there is less hair left, and the remaining hairs need more time to enter the active phase. Coming in early means the session is largely wasted.
Is the result permanent?
A treated follicle does not recover. But the skin holds follicles that were dormant during the course, and hormones can switch them on — pregnancy, thyroid, medication. Then single maintenance sessions are enough, not a new course.
Do you treat a woman's face with laser?
No. Facial hair in women is more often hormone-driven, and the laser can stimulate growth in that area. For the face we offer electrolysis, which treats each hair individually.
Can it be done in summer?
Yes, if the area is covered by clothing and you are not tanning. For exposed areas — legs, arms, shoulders — summer means SPF and no tanning for two weeks before and after a session.
What about ingrown hairs?
They usually go along with the course: the hair stops growing, so there is nothing to grow in. Early in the course a gentle scrub helps, as does swapping the razor for a trimmer between sessions.

How to choose a hair-removal specialist

You recognise a good specialist by the questions they ask you, not by photos of the machine. Here is what to check before you lie down on the couch.

They ask about your health

Before the first pulse the specialist must ask about hormonal conditions, pregnancy, any medication you take, sun exposure in the past two weeks and reactions to previous treatments. If there was no form and no conversation and you are sent straight to the couch, that is not saving your time — it is a burn risk.

Settings are chosen for you

One setting for everybody does not work: the energy depends on your skin phototype, on hair thickness and colour, on the area and even on the season. Ask which settings will be used on your area and why. A sound answer is specific; “we have a standard programme” is a reason to be wary.

You are told the number of sessions and the intervals

Before the course starts you should know how many sessions are planned, how many weeks apart to come, and what happens if you miss one. A promise of “gone forever in three visits” is untrue: hair grows in cycles, and one session only treats the hairs currently in the growth phase.

They show their own work and mention the unpleasant parts

Ask for before-and-after photos of their own clients, not pictures from the manufacturer. And listen to how they describe the sensation: an honest specialist will say that the bikini line and underarms are more sensitive, that redness for a couple of hours is normal, and will explain what not to do for the first day.
